## Abstract

This thesis develops a manifestly causal approach to quantum field theory (QFT) calculations, introducing new probability-level formalisms that maintain causality throughout the calculation process. The framework is applied to study quantum field theory in curved spacetime, with particular focus on the Unruh effect and quantum information in black hole spacetimes. The work bridges fundamental theoretical physics with potential applications in quantum computing and quantum information theory.

## Layman's Abstract

Quantum field theory describes how particles and fields behave at the smallest scales, but traditional methods can obscure the fundamental principle of causality – that effects must follow their causes. This thesis develops new mathematical techniques that keep causality explicit throughout calculations, making the physics clearer and potentially easier to simulate on quantum computers. The methods are applied to understand how quantum fields behave near black holes and for accelerated observers, revealing new insights into how quantum information behaves in extreme gravitational settings.
