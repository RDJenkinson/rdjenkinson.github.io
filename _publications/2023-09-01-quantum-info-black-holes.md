## Abstract

Black holes present fundamental challenges to our understanding of quantum information and its relationship with gravity. Using manifestly causal probability-level formalisms, we investigate how quantum information behaves in black hole spacetimes, with particular focus on Hawking radiation and information preservation. Our causal framework provides new perspectives on the black hole information paradox, suggesting how quantum information might be encoded in Hawking radiation while maintaining consistency with general relativity and quantum mechanics.

## Layman's Abstract

Black holes are regions of space where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape. When quantum mechanics is applied to black holes, Stephen Hawking showed they should slowly evaporate by emitting radiation. But this creates a puzzle: what happens to the information that falls into a black hole? This paper uses new mathematical techniques that explicitly respect causality to study how quantum information behaves near black holes, potentially helping resolve this fundamental paradox and understand how information might be preserved in Hawking radiation.
