## Abstract

We present a framework for simulating quantum field theory (QFT) processes on quantum computers using manifestly causal probability-level formalisms. By maintaining explicit causality throughout the simulation, we enable more efficient qubit-based representations of QFT phenomena. The approach is demonstrated through simulations of particle scattering processes and quantum field evolution in flat spacetime, showing improved computational efficiency compared to traditional methods. This work bridges theoretical quantum field theory with practical quantum computing applications.

## Layman's Abstract

Quantum computers use "qubits" – quantum bits that can exist in multiple states simultaneously – to perform calculations impossible on classical computers. This paper shows how to use quantum computers to simulate the behavior of quantum fields, which describe particles and forces at the smallest scales. By keeping the fundamental principle of causality (cause and effect) explicit in our calculations, we can make these simulations more efficient and easier to run on real quantum computers. This could help us understand fundamental physics better and develop new quantum technologies.
