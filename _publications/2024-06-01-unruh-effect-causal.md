## Abstract

The Unruh effect demonstrates that an accelerated observer perceives the quantum vacuum as a thermal bath of particles, revealing deep connections between acceleration, temperature, and quantum field theory. We reformulate the analysis of this effect using manifestly causal probability-level formalisms, providing new insights into how causality constrains quantum field theory in accelerated frames. Our approach clarifies the relationship between the Unruh effect and other quantum field phenomena, and suggests pathways for experimental verification using quantum simulators.

## Layman's Abstract

According to Einstein's theory of relativity, an observer accelerating through empty space will detect particles and heat, even though a stationary observer sees nothing. This strange effect, called the Unruh effect, reveals deep connections between motion, temperature, and quantum physics. This paper uses new mathematical techniques that keep the principle of causality (cause and effect) explicit to study this effect, making the physics clearer and potentially easier to test in experiments using quantum computers.
